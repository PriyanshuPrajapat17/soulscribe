This is why you're feeling stuck in your spiritual wellbeing

You're chanting your rounds. You're reading the Gita. You're serving in the temple kitchen on Sundays. You attend lectures, take notes, nod along.

And yet—if you're honest—something still feels... stuck.

The inspiration from the morning lecture fades by lunch. The realization you had during kirtan? Gone by dinner. You're doing everything right, but your spiritual life feels like it's moving in slow motion. Like you're running on a treadmill—effort, but no progress.

Here's what might be missing: The Missing Link Between Hearing and Knowing

In Vedanta, there's a beautiful framework for how real transformation happens. It's called Shravanam, Mananam, Nididhyasanam.

  • • Shravanam is hearing. Listening to the wisdom—from your guru, from scriptures, from satsang.
  • • Mananam is reflecting. Turning it over in your mind. Questioning it. Digesting it until it becomes your understanding, not just borrowed knowledge.
  • • Nididhyasanam is absorbing. Meditating on it until it seeps into your consciousness and starts shaping how you live, react, and love.

Think of it like eating:

Hearing = taking a bite

Reflecting = chewing and digesting

Absorbing = letting the nutrients become part of you

Most of us stop at the first step. We hear. We collect. We accumulate wisdom like library books we never open. But without reflection and absorption, the wisdom stays outside us. It never becomes ours.

That's why you can chant your rounds and still feel disconnected. You're hearing the words, but not letting them settle into your heart.

A Simple Practice to Move from Hearing to Absorbing

Next time you hear a lecture or read a page from the Gita, try this gentle journaling practice. Ask yourself three questions:

✍️ What can I recall? Not everything—just one thing that touched you. One line. One idea. Write it down in your own words.
✍️ What can I relish? What felt sweet about it? What resonated? Maybe it gave you comfort, or challenged you, or sparked curiosity. Let yourself savor it on the page.
✍️ What can I act on? This is where absorption begins. What's one small thing you can do—today, this week—to live that wisdom? Not a grand gesture. Just one step.

That's it. One page. Three questions. Five minutes.

And suddenly, you're not just hearing anymore. You're digesting. You're absorbing. You're moving from Shravanam to Mananam to Nididhyasanam—one small journal entry at a time.

Because spiritual growth isn't about how much wisdom you collect. It's about how much of it becomes you.
